### Added — Parky reads the whole weather: chance of rain, rain hours, wind and gusts, UV, sunshine and the warnings in force

Parky knew a day's condition, its temperatures and how many millimetres fall, and
nothing else. park.fan did not fetch a daily chance of rain at all, nor the gusts,
and the wind, snow and feels-like the calendar did hold never reached his tools.
`get_park_day` handed him the calendar block whole, legacy `rainChance` included:
millimetres under a name that reads as a percentage, which he said as
"12,6 % Regenwahrscheinlichkeit" for 12.6 mm.

**Fetched and stored.** The daily Open-Meteo request adds
`precipitation_probability_max`, `precipitation_hours`, `wind_gusts_10m_max`,
`uv_index_max` and `sunshine_duration`, stored in five new nullable `weather_data`
columns (`precipitationProbabilityMax`, `precipitationHours`, `windGustsMax`,
`uvIndexMax`, `sunshineHours`). The calendar's weather block, the park payload's
weather and the [daily weather rows](frontend/weather.md) carry them where a row
has them, never as 0. The hourly request adds `precipitation_probability` and
`wind_gusts_10m`; the ML service's model reads neither. Each forecast call now
weighs about two calls against Open-Meteo's free allowance instead of about one
and a half ([weather](architecture/weather.md)).

**One weather view for the tools.** `weatherView` gives `get_days`, `get_park_day`
and the admin MCP's `park_forecast` every figure with its unit
(`precipitationChancePercent`, `windGustsMaxKmh`, …) and leaves `rainChance` out.
`get_park_weather` adds wind, gusts and humidity now, hail and storm times, the
official severe-weather warnings in force, and each coming hour's chance of rain
and gusts.

**Tip and prompt.** The day tip (prompt version 6) gets the chance of rain, the
hours with precipitation and the gusts; it may say the chance as given, never
millimetres as a percentage, and wind only when the gusts are stormy. Parky's
prompt (version 62) says which tool holds which weather, that the amount is never
the chance, and that stormy gusts or a warning can stop tall rides, never that
they will. One eval case covers the mix-up.
