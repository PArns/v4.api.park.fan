### Fixed — TimescaleDB jobs no longer queue every reader behind an ACCESS EXCLUSIVE lock

The API was unreachable on 2026-10-09 from 02:12 to 07:31 UTC and stalled in
five-minute blocks on 10-07 and 10-08, all three times behind a TimescaleDB
background job waiting for a lock (runbook §0b). The retention policy on
`wait_time_predictions` locks `attractions` ACCESS EXCLUSIVE on every run —
`drop_chunks` locks each table the hypertable has a foreign key to, before it
even looks for a chunk — and 655 queries, every one of them on `attractions`,
finished in the very seconds its 20 attempts gave up. The `queue_data`
compression job's final truncate waited five hours for the same kind of lock on
a 30-day-old chunk.

The retention policy is removed at boot, and the nightly `cleanup-old` job now
drops chunks past 90 days itself: only when `show_chunks` says one is due, and
under a 2-second `lock_timeout`, so a busy table means "try tomorrow" instead of
a queue. Compression runs with `compress_truncate_behaviour = truncate_or_delete`,
which tries the truncate lock without queueing and falls back to a delete. Every
service now connects with an `application_name` and a 10-minute
`idle_in_transaction_session_timeout`, and Postgres logs lock waits, because the
session that held the lock for hours could not be identified afterwards.
