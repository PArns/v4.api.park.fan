### Added — NeuralForecast candidates (TiDE, NHITS, TSMixerx) in ml-bench

`ml-bench` can score three supervised global deep models with known-future
covariates against the benchmark's baselines: an hourly operating-day panel per
ride (closed hours and days stay on the grid with `available_mask = 0`), trained
once per month block on data before the block's first origin
(`mlbench/models/nf_precompute.py`, GPU), forecast d0–d7 from every daily and
intraday origin, and spread over the 15-minute slots with H5's within-hour shape
(`mlbench/models/neuralforecast_models.py`). The precompute holds the shared GPU
lock, caps its share of the shared GPU's memory, sizes its predict batches from the
number of series so one setting covers a park subset and all 157 parks, and refuses
to start a block whose projected finish would reach into the host's nightly window.
The panel's known-future covariates deliberately exclude the schedule's own holiday and
bridge-day annotations, which cannot be shown to be origin-safe from the export. Scoring
can pair a candidate against a named non-reference baseline (`MLBENCH_EXTRA_PAIR_REFS`,
opt-in and inert by default), because the incumbents to beat — `lvlh5_tft` and `h5` — are
not reference candidates and a paired intersection cannot be reconstructed after a run.
The panel, the information cut and the cost of a full run are documented in
`docs/ml/ml-bench.md` (PAR-829).

The precompute sizes its own training-window pool. NeuralForecast materialises the whole
unfolded window pool before `windows_batch_size` samples from it, so the peak is
`batch_size x ceil(T_train / step_size) x (input_size + h) x n_cols x 4` bytes and does
not depend on `windows_batch_size` at all; at the default step of 1 that is 14 GiB for a
late block and exceeded the job's share of the shared GPU. The unfold step is now derived
from a VRAM budget and kept coprime with the hourly grid so the retained windows still
cover every hour-of-day phase. It is a training-only knob: forecasting uses
`predict_step_size`, which the precompute sets explicitly per predict call, so the
thinning cannot change a forecast.

Rides with less than a day of measured history in a block's training region are excluded
from that block's fit and kept in its forecasts. NeuralForecast keeps a training window
only if it has an available step in both the insample and the horizon, so a ride with a
single available step yields no usable window and a whole batch of such rides aborts the
block; a ride that is too sparse to train on at the block cutoff may still be well
measured by a later origin, so dropping it from the forecasts would quietly cut coverage.

Blocks are written to temporary files, verified, and renamed into place, so an interrupted
precompute can never leave a block that a later resumed pass accepts as complete. The
cached `attraction_id` is a parquet dictionary column rather than a fixed-width string
array, which is what keeps a late block's rows inside the job's memory cap.


Four read-only analysis tools sit beside the harness and are the only source of the
figures this workstream publishes. `par829_paired_margin.py` reports a candidate's
paired margin against each *named* baseline — the incumbents a model actually has to
beat are `lvlh5_tft` and `h5`, and neither is a reference candidate — with both the
park-day and the park-cluster bootstrap interval, the number of bootstrap units behind
each difference, and the paired share of the baseline's covered slots next to every
margin, on the daily slot part and on the intraday part. `par829_staleness.py` measures
the slope of a candidate's margin against the age of its cached weights instead of
asserting which way that bias runs; the candidate's weights are up to 31 days stale
inside a month block while `h5`, `wt_med` and `clim` are recomputed at every origin, and
the sign of that asymmetry is not established, so these margins must not be read as a
conservative lower bound. `par829_coverage.py` prints the exact asymmetric coverage per
arm, the composed-level slot-count parity gate, and a **weekday census per lead** derived
from the run's own output: no table in the harness carries a weekday column, which is how
a strided origin subset elsewhere in this review aliased its entire lead axis with
day-of-week for two days without anyone noticing. `par829_tables.py` and
`par829_verdict.py` turn those CSVs into the published markdown tables and apply the
pre-registered decision rule as code, so no published number is hand-transcribed; both
refuse to emit rather than print an empty section when their inputs are absent.

The three candidates are now scored. On 61 consecutive daily origins over the autumn
shoulder, all 157 parks, TiDE beats the live incumbents — the current wait, the hour-5
profile and the production TFT level — on intraday origins from 60 to 120 minutes ahead on
busy rides and from 90 minutes ahead on all rides, and on the two-to-eight-hour bands; the
current wait still wins the first 45 minutes outright, so the usable region is a band and
not a horizon prefix. On the planner's 15-minute slots from the daily origin the production
TFT level is better at every lead d0 to d7 in both segments, so nothing here replaces it,
and perfect weather foreknowledge closes only a small fraction of that gap. The daily level
these models produce is worse than a seasonal-naive level on the crowd calendar, the crowd
bucket and the day comparison. NHITS wins on the live surface only at the longest leads and
is behind TiDE wherever both were scored; TSMixerx was not scored at all, only costed. Every
recommendation is capped at shadow, because the decision rule fixed before scoring requires
the sign to agree across seasons and this run has autumn origins only.

A full nine-block precompute over all 157 parks costs about a quarter of an hour of GPU per
model and peaks at 2.5 GB of device memory, against the 29 to 36 hours the plan assumed; the
binding constraint is the single CPU scoring token, not the device. The horizon is limited to
eight days by the configured forecast length, not by budget.
