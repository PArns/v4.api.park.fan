### Fixed — a day the schedule source withdraws stops being an operating day

`saveScheduleData` worked from the days the payload **contains**, so a day
ThemeParks.wiki stopped naming was never looked at: no `UPDATE`, no `DELETE`. The
row kept saying OPERATING until the visit date passed, and the calendar, the day
planner and best-days all offered it as a visit day. `fillScheduleGaps` could not
catch it either — it fills missing dates and demotes its own gap-filled CLOSED
rows, and deliberately never touches an OPERATING row from the API.

Measured on production on 2026-09-28, over the ten parks with the most future
OPERATING rows, for 2026-11, 2026-12 and 2027-01: **18 wrong days in 2 of 10
parks**. Rulantica held 14 — its annual maintenance closure, 2026-11-16 to 27,
plus 24 and 25 December, all written on 2026-02-11 and never touched again, with
the upstream naming none of those days in any form. SeaWorld San Antonio held 4.

The reason this could not simply be "delete what the payload does not name" is
the run where the source answers badly. `getScheduleExtended` asks for 13 months
one at a time and returns one flat array, so a month that dropped a day and a
month that never arrived look identical from the outside — and on a throttled run
87 of 200 parks come away with nothing (PAR-480). In the same measurement
Blackpool Pleasure Beach answered empty for two months and SeaWorld Orlando's
December failed outright; a naive comparison would have deleted 31 real operating
days.

So the client now reports which months answered (`coveredMonths`: 200 **and** at
least one entry, from the month endpoint only), and the retraction is confined to
them, to park-level rows, to OPERATING, to days after the park's own today, and
to days the payload does not name as operating. The end state is left to the
`fillScheduleGaps` pass that already runs immediately after — CLOSED between two
operating days, UNKNOWN past the last one. Hand-written CLOSED rows are out of
reach by construction: `schedule_entries` has no provenance column, and
Phantasialand's 2027-01-12/13 (`Closed per park announcement (PAR-532)`) sit on
dates the upstream no longer names inside a month it answers for.
