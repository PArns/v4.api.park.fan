### Fixed — the failing-job sweep reads every queue, and a deploy no longer erases the failures

The nightly data-quality sweep read Bull's failed sets from a hand-written list
of 14 queues whose comment called it "every queue this app registers". The app
registers 32. The other 18 (`downtime`, `p50-baseline`, `park-day-operation`,
`attraction-hourly-history`, `rope-drop`, `typical-waits`, `pcn-shadow`,
`nf-training`, `shape-shadow`, `show-patterns`, `trips`, `push-notifications`,
`weather-warnings`, `weather-historical`, `wartezeiten-schedule`,
`six-flags-heights`, `geoip-update`, `ride-stats`) were never read, which is
how four days of `downtime` failures went by without a line in the log.
`MONITORED_QUEUES` is now derived from `BULL_QUEUE_REGISTRATIONS`; leaving a
queue out takes an entry in `UNMONITORED_QUEUES` with a reason, and a spec fails
for a registered queue that is in neither.

The bootstrap also ran `queue.clean(0, "completed"|"failed", 1000)` on
`wait-times`, `park-metadata`, `children-metadata`, `weather`, `holidays` and
`ml-training` at every start, so each deploy deleted the evidence the sweep
reads. Retention was already bounded by `removeOnComplete: 100` /
`removeOnFail: 500`; the wipe is gone.

Without the wipe, an old failure would sit in the last 500 and be reported every
night. The sweep therefore only counts a failure until its job has had its next
scheduled run: at least 26 h, and for a slower cron back to its previous fire,
read from the queue's repeat ZSET (`failureWindowStarts`), so a weekly or
monthly job's failure stays visible until it runs again.
