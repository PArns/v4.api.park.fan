### Performance — the site statistic's long windows read daily sums

On the synthetic year at ten times today's traffic the Traffic page, sixteen reports at once over 365
days, took 17.6 s on four CPUs (8.4 s under one filter, 10.2 s under a visit filter) and now takes 2.9 s
(2.6 s and 4.9 s). The new nightly job `site-analytics` / `rollup-days` (02:35, 08:35, 14:35 and 20:35
UTC) sums each finished UTC day into two insert-only hypertables, `site_pageviews_daily` and
`site_events_daily`, in 40 groupings of the day by a few dimensions (cuboids) for page views and four
for events, and writes a marker per cuboid and day in `site_rollup_days` together with its rows. A
report takes the smallest cuboid that holds the columns it reads, whole rolled days from it and today's
hours (and the hour a window before ends at) from the hourly rows, and answers exactly what the
hourly rows give: 1,127 answers on the synthetic year, every report, window length, filter and
language, compared both ways, none differed. Lists that tied on count were ordered by whatever the
`GROUP BY` returned; they are ordered by name now, so no plan changes an answer. A report that no
cuboid can serve (a filter on the path or the entry page, three filters) reads the hourly rows as
before, and every statement now runs with one hash table over all chunks, which was two to three
times faster there too. The tables compress 10× and are dropped with the hourly chunks after 400
days: a year takes 1.05 GB at ten times today's traffic, 0.52 of it the hourly rows. Nothing is
updated or deleted (0 dead tuples after the 365-day backfill, 1.1 s a day to write) and `synchronize`
finds nothing to change. Continuous aggregates were measured and left: one view over every dimension
does not shrink the data, and a view blocks dropping the columns of its source. See
[site statistic](frontend/site-analytics.md#the-daily-level).

### Added — the admin's statistic reports are bounded: four at a time, 15 s a statement, 503 past it

Every report of the site statistic, the admin's and its MCP tools', runs through `SiteReportRunner`:
at most four at once per process (`SITE_ANALYTICS_REPORT_CONCURRENCY`), 32 more may wait up to 30 s
(`SITE_ANALYTICS_REPORT_QUEUE`, `SITE_ANALYTICS_REPORT_QUEUE_WAIT_MS`), and each statement is
cancelled after 15 s (`SITE_ANALYTICS_STATEMENT_TIMEOUT_MS`) with 32 MB for its sorts and hashes
(`SITE_ANALYTICS_REPORT_WORK_MEM_MB`), set for that statement's transaction only. A report that waits
too long, finds the queue full or is cancelled answers 503 with `{ statusCode, error, reason, message }`
(`reason` is `busy` or `timeout`; the message says what to try). The Traffic page's sixteen
reports now queue behind four instead of filling the pool. See
[bounds](frontend/site-analytics.md#reports).

### Changed — an hourly series of a past day has its visitors

`site_visitor_hours` (new, 168 rows a day, insert-only) keeps each hour's unique visitors, written by
the same four `snapshot-visitors` runs after midnight from the hour HyperLogLogs, which now live an
hour past their day (`uvh`, as the day's own: at most 25 h). `interval=hour` reports `visitors` for
every hour of a finished day, where it used to be `null`; hours of days before this deploy stay
`null`. No other response changes. See
[visitors](frontend/site-analytics.md#visits-visitors-bounce-realtime).
