### Fixed — one unwritable child no longer costs its park the whole children sync

The three sync loops in `handleFetchChildren` ran bare inside the park-wide `try/catch`, so the first child that threw ended the park: every child after it, the reclassification and absence steps, the mapping job and the cache eviction all went with it. Nothing in the run said which park had been cut short, because the park-wide handler logs the error and returns zeros.

Two parks had been losing almost all of their feed to this for 71 days. Knott's Soak City wrote one of nine rows per run and the other eight last on 2026-07-26; Cedar Point Shores wrote two of seventeen and the other fifteen last on 2026-07-27. Both have the same shape: the child at feed index 1 is an entity the wiki lists under both parks of the resort, and its row already exists in the sibling park. `attractions.externalId` carries a global unique index rather than a park-scoped one, so the insert for the second park can never succeed — and it threw on every single run, at the same place, right after the child at index 0 had been written.

Each child is now written inside its own `try/catch`, in all three loops and in the Queue-Times fallback beside them. A child that cannot be written is skipped with its park, its name, its id and the error, the park keeps the rest of its feed and all of its follow-up steps, and the number of skipped children is counted per park and for the run. The skip is never silent: it means a listed child has no row this run.

The absence step now runs for such a park where the throw used to prevent it, and it cannot read the skipped child as gone — every id the response carried is in `listedExternalIds` whatever happened to its row. That the sibling parks of a resort compete for one globally unique `externalId` is a separate data conflict and is not addressed here.
