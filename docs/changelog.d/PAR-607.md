### Changed — a ride retired as closed keeps its page, its sitemap entry and a place on its park page

X2 at Six Flags Magic Mountain was retired on 2026-07-13 with a news post as its
reason. Its detail endpoint kept answering, as the `retiredAt` docstring promises,
but the frontend resolved the ride page from the park payload, which drops retired
rides, and served a 404. The attraction sitemap had been left without them on
2026-09-24 for the same reason (70 of 7,374 rows then). The frontend now renders
such a page as closed permanently from the detail endpoint, so the URL is listed
again.

The attraction response carries `retiredKind`: `closed` for a retirement a human
entered, `reclassified` for one the children sync wrote because ThemeParks.wiki
now lists the entity as a show or a restaurant, and null while not retired.
Before this a client could only tell the two apart by matching
`RECLASSIFIED_UPSTREAM_REASON` word for word. `retiredKindOf()` is the one rule;
the sitemap and the DTO both call it.

The sitemap lists `closed` rides and leaves `reclassified` rows out. Live rows are
still grouped by name exactly as the park payload groups them, and a closed row
never enters those groups: where a live ride in the same park carries the same
name, the live ride keeps the URL. The cache key moves to
`sitemap:attractions:v4` so the list changes on deploy.

The park payload carries `closedAttractions`: the park's rides retired as
`closed`, newest closure first, each with its name, slug, detail path, themed
area and `retiredAt`. It is a list of its own and not part of `attractions`, so
no count, filter or planner reads a closed ride as part of the park today. The
same name rule applies as in the sitemap. `buildClosedAttractions` builds it,
`AttractionsService.findClosedForParkPage` reads the rows, and a failed read
costs the list and nothing else.

New column `attractions.retired_hidden` (default false, created by
`synchronize`). It takes a closed ride off its park page and nothing else: the
ride page and the sitemap entry stay, because a URL's ranking does not expire
with the visitor's interest in the park's history. Set through
`POST /admin/retired-attractions/:id/hidden` with `{ "hidden": true|false }`
(owner), refused for a row that is not retired, cleared by an un-retirement.
`GET /admin/retired-attractions` now returns `kind` and `hidden` per row.

A closed ride leaves its park page a year after it closed:
`closedAttractions` holds it for `CLOSED_RIDE_PARK_PAGE_DAYS` (365) after its
`retiredAt`, after which it answers only on its own URL; its page and its
sitemap entry stay. `retired_hidden` still takes a ride off sooner.
`isOnParkPage()` is the one rule: `findClosedForParkPage` applies it (the query
asks for `retiredAt > now − 365 days` and not hidden), and
`GET /admin/retired-attractions` reports it per row as `onParkPage`. A ride
crossing the year drops out with the next rebuild of the park's cached payload,
at most six hours for a shut park.
