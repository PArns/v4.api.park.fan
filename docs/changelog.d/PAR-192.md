### Fixed — a park whose feed has been silent for a month stops reporting every ride as operating

La Ronde had produced no live reading since 2026-06-24 (Queue-Times) and
2026-06-17 (ThemeParks.wiki) — 84 and 91 days on 2026-09-16. Both upstreams
still answer and both answer empty. The park page served all **38** attractions
as `effectiveStatus: OPERATING` at `crowdLevel: very_low`, while each ride's own
endpoint served `CLOSED` off the same silence and the hourly forecast predicted
30 minutes at 93.6 % confidence.

The optimistic fallback in `statusWithoutLiveData` is written for **one** ride
going quiet at a park whose feed works; its own docblock names the symptom it
was added to stop — "one ride, one request apart, two answers" — and a park
where every ride is quiet reproduces exactly that, 38 times over.

`ParkIntegrationService` now reads
`waitTimesKnowable = waitTimesReadable && parkObservedRecently`. The second half
is `QueueDataService.hasObservedReadingWithin(parkId, PARK_FEED_SILENT_DAYS)`, a
bounded `EXISTS` over `observedReadingsSql()` so our own reconciliation and
heartbeat rows cannot clear a park. The four surfaces that already read
`waitTimesReadable` — ride status, crowd level, best visit times, the park's wait
statistics — read the pair instead, and a silent park's rides land on `UNKNOWN`,
the same place a park with no readable source sends them.

`liveWaitTimes.available` stays `true`. It is the frontend's contract for "this
park publishes wait times nowhere", and `parks/data/live-wait-time-sources.ts`
says in its header that the list must not be derived; saying it of a park that
fed us 7.307 rows through June would be a different wrong answer.

**It is a class of five, not the single case the ticket found.** Counting parks
that have *never* produced a reading — which a `max(timestamp) < cutoff`
comparison drops — 2026-09-16 holds La Ronde (38 rides), Movieland The Hollywood
Park (25), Water Country USA (19), Adventure Island Tampa (16) and Paradise
Country (12): 110 attractions with 25 scheduled open days in the following week.
Four of them read `CLOSED` in that snapshot only because they were outside their
own opening hours at that minute.

### Added — a detector for a park the schedule says is open and the feed says nothing about

`DataQualityMonitorService.findScheduledButSilentParks()` is the complement of
`findSilencedClusters`, which cannot be widened to cover it: that query's
`park_health` CTE demands at least three attractions with an `OPERATING` reading
in the last two days, exactly so a park closing for the season is not read as a
dropped feed — so the detector built to find dropped feeds is blind to the
largest drop there is. What separates the two cases here is the schedule, not
the feed: a park shut for the winter publishes no future `OPERATING` day either.

Unlike the cluster detector it has no window and does not go quiet by itself.
That is the difference in kind. A silenced cluster resolves into a judgement
("that was the water park closing") which leaves no trace in the data, so a
detector that kept firing would ask the same answered question every night. This
state has two exits and both are edits — the feed returns, or the schedule stops
claiming days nobody can confirm.

Measured against production on 2026-09-16: the detector runs in 1.2 s and
returns the five parks above. The per-request probe costs 1.8 ms at Europa-Park,
where the first row short-circuits the `LIMIT`, and 7.0 ms at La Ronde, where all
38 index searches come back empty, behind 45 ms of hypertable planning on a warm
backend and 338 ms on a cold one — the second paid once per pooled connection,
and in the same band as `findCurrentStatusByPark` beside it.

`test/e2e/scheduled-but-silent-parks.e2e-spec.ts` runs both against a real
PostgreSQL. Every case is a pair: the silent park and the healthy one differ in a
single `queue_data` row, and the healthy one has to be absent from the same
result the silent one is present in — an assertion about an absence alone stays
green against a detector that returns nothing at all.
