### Fixed — a park whose feed has been silent for a month stops reporting every ride as operating

La Ronde had produced no live reading since 2026-06-24 (Queue-Times) and
2026-06-17 (ThemeParks.wiki) — 84 and 91 days on 2026-09-16. Both upstreams
still answer and both answer empty. The park page served all **38** attractions
as `effectiveStatus: OPERATING` at `crowdLevel: very_low`, while each ride's own
endpoint served `CLOSED` off the same silence and the hourly forecast predicted
30 minutes at 93.6 % confidence.

The optimistic fallback in `statusWithoutLiveData` is written for **one** ride
going quiet at a park whose feed works; its own docblock names the symptom it
was added to stop — "one ride, one request apart, two answers" — and a park
where every ride is quiet reproduces exactly that, 38 times over.

`ParkIntegrationService` now reads
`waitTimesKnowable = waitTimesReadable && parkObservedRecently`. The second half
is `QueueDataService.hasObservedReadingWithin(parkId, PARK_FEED_SILENT_DAYS)`, a
bounded `EXISTS` over `observedReadingsSql()` so our own reconciliation and
heartbeat rows cannot clear a park. The four surfaces that already read
`waitTimesReadable` — ride status, crowd level, best visit times, the park's wait
statistics — read the pair instead, and a silent park's rides land on `UNKNOWN`,
the same place a park with no readable source sends them.

`closedAttractions` reads 0 in that branch **while the park itself is open**.
The comment defending those counters said "0 operating is true, none is *known*
to run", which covers one half; the other was computed as `total - operating`
and served "38 of 38 closed" under an `OPERATING` park badge — the same false
page from the other direction, and already reachable for a curated
no-wait-times park before this change. In a CLOSED park the rides really are
closed, so the count stands there. `operatingAttractions` is left to compute
itself rather than forced to 0: at a silent park it counts the free-flow rides,
which are open on a curated flag and the schedule rather than on a wait time.

`total = operating + closed` therefore does not hold at an open park whose waits
are unknowable, and three DTO descriptions say so. `liveWaitTimes.available`
gets the same treatment: its description named itself as the tell for "these
numbers are an aggregate over nothing", and a silent park keeps the flag `true`,
so it now says that `true` is a statement about the source and not about the
last thirty days.

**Thirty days, measured against a distribution rather than an incident.** Days
since the last observed reading, per park with at least one un-retired
attraction, 2026-09-16: **195** parks at 0–1 days, **0** at 2–30, 2 at 31–90, 3
at 90+, 4 never seen in 400 days. The band this threshold sits in is empty, at
fifteen times the maximum a working feed produces. Busch Gardens Tampa's 65-day
recovery is not the comparison — that was nine rides of a park whose feed kept
working, which is `findSilencedClusters`' subject and never reaches this rule.

`liveWaitTimes.available` stays `true`. It is the frontend's contract for "this
park publishes wait times nowhere", and `parks/data/live-wait-time-sources.ts`
says in its header that the list must not be derived; saying it of a park that
fed us 7.307 rows through June would be a different wrong answer.

**Three surfaces still gate on `waitTimesReadable` alone** and are deliberately
left for their own issue: the ride's own endpoint, the favourites list and
`/location`. A silent park's ride therefore still answers `CLOSED` on its own
page and still carries its hourly forecast — a narrower contradiction than the
one removed (`UNKNOWN` against `CLOSED` rather than `OPERATING` against
`CLOSED`), but a contradiction. Each of those paths needs its own park-level
probe, which is more than this issue asked for.

**It is a class of five, not the single case the ticket found.** Counting parks
that have *never* produced a reading — which a `max(timestamp) < cutoff`
comparison drops — 2026-09-16 holds La Ronde (38 rides), Movieland The Hollywood
Park (25), Water Country USA (19), Adventure Island Tampa (16) and Paradise
Country (12): 110 attractions with 25 scheduled open days in the following week.
Four of them read `CLOSED` in that snapshot only because they were outside their
own opening hours at that minute.

### Added — a detector for a park the schedule says is open and the feed says nothing about

`DataQualityMonitorService.findScheduledButSilentParks()` is the complement of
`findSilencedClusters`, which cannot be widened to cover it: that query's
`park_health` CTE demands at least three attractions with an `OPERATING` reading
in the last two days, exactly so a park closing for the season is not read as a
dropped feed — so the detector built to find dropped feeds is blind to the
largest drop there is. What separates the two cases here is the schedule, not
the feed: a park shut for the winter publishes no future `OPERATING` day either.

It reads the schedule over the next `SILENT_PARK_LOOKAHEAD_DAYS` = 7 days rather
than over every published day. Without that window it is a seasonal-park alarm:
a park shut for the winter with next summer already on the calendar has future
operating days and an empty feed, and is neither a fault nor news. At 7, 30 and
unbounded the query returns the same five parks today, so the window costs
nothing now and is what keeps January out of the log.

Unlike the cluster detector it does not go quiet by itself, and that is the
difference in kind. A silenced cluster resolves into a judgement ("that was the
water park closing") which leaves no trace in the data, so a detector that kept
firing would ask the same answered question every night. This state has two
exits and both are edits — the feed returns, or the schedule stops claiming days
nobody can confirm.

Measured against production on 2026-09-16: the detector runs in 1.2 s and
returns the five parks above. The per-request probe costs 1.8 ms at Europa-Park,
where the first row short-circuits the `LIMIT`, and 7.0 ms at La Ronde, where all
38 index searches come back empty, behind 45 ms of hypertable planning on a warm
backend and 338 ms on a cold one — the second paid once per pooled connection,
and in the same band as `findCurrentStatusByPark` beside it.

Each of the three detectors now catches its own throw, and the job counts how
many ran. A `Promise.all` that rejects takes the other two down with it — the
failure mode this module exists for, since `detect-seasonal` threw on every run
for 73 days and nothing said so. The catch alone is not the whole fix: three
caught throws leave three empty lists, and an empty list is what "nothing is
wrong" looks like, so the clean ✅ would have printed directly under three ERROR
lines. Below three checks the job says `inconclusive: N of 3 checks ran`.

`test/e2e/scheduled-but-silent-parks.e2e-spec.ts` runs the detector, the probe
**and the park payload itself** against a real PostgreSQL. Every case is a pair:
the two parks differ in a single `queue_data` row, and one has to be absent from
the same result the other is present in — an assertion about an absence alone
stays green against a query that returns nothing at all. The two HTTP cases are
the same construction one level up, and they are what makes the behaviour half
of this change testable at all: delete `&& parkObservedRecently` and the two
responses become identical.

Every gate was checked by mutation rather than by reading: removing the date
comparison, the `attractionId` filter, the lookahead window, `observedReadingsSql`,
the anti-join or the measured half of `waitTimesKnowable` turns exactly one case
red each. The "schedule has run out" fixture originally seeded no schedule rows
at all, so it left the join for two reasons and survived the first of those
mutations; it now seeds past operating days.
