### Added — `/predictions/yearly` lists the park's holidays for the year ahead

The yearly response has a new `holidays` list: every day from the park's today
through 364 days ahead that is a public holiday, a school-break day or a bridge
day in the park's own region, with `holidayName` and `schoolVacationName` in
English where a holiday row names the day. The park page's twelve-month outlook
draws school breaks and public holidays from it. Until now only `/calendar` had
these flags, and it answers at most 90 days per request at about 50 KB a month.
At Phantasialand the calendar marks 41 school-break days and 7 public holidays
in the 182 days the forecast covers.

It is a list of its own rather than flags on the forecast days. The forecast
skips the days it has nothing for, Christmas Day at Phantasialand among them,
and stops about 182 days out, while next summer's school break is already known.
If the holiday table cannot be read, or the park has no country, `holidays` is
absent; an empty list means the year has none.

The rule is the calendar's own. The local-holiday test and the per-day holiday
map moved out of `CalendarService` into `isLocalHoliday()` and
`buildLocalHolidayMap()` (`src/common/utils/holiday.utils.ts`); the calendar
and `ParkIntegrationService.getYearlyHolidays()` both read them, and
`dayHolidayFlags()` runs `calculateHolidayInfo()` over the year. The holidays
are read in one `getHolidays` call, three days past both ends, because a break
absorbs a weekend by looking across it to the Thursday or the Tuesday.

Two things changed for the calendar along the way. A holiday row is now keyed
by the day it names (`holidayDay()`), not by noon UTC read in the park's zone,
which put every holiday a day late for parks at UTC+12 and beyond: Christmas
landed on the 26th at Rainbow's End in Auckland. And the local-holiday test
normalises each distinct region once per park instead of twice per row.

Cost of the new list, measured on the built module with a year of holidays in
blocks: 18–25 ms for a German park (2,936 rows), 54–79 ms for a Dutch one, whose
school breaks are stored per municipality (257,734 rows). The first version took
33 and 497 ms. The live endpoint answered Phantasialand in 0.37–0.59 s before
the change.
