### Fixed — a ride whose source stopped measuring it reads UNKNOWN instead of CLOSED

A feed can keep answering about a ride and stop looking at it, and until now the
two were published identically. On 2026-10-27 LEGOLAND Billund's feed delivered a
row for all 37 of its attractions at 09:15 UTC carrying `lastUpdated = 2026-04-06
13:54:00+00` on every one of them, identical to the second and 203 days old. The
park was open, and all 37 read CLOSED as a statement of fact — a stamp from April
served as the operator's word in October. The reverse-reconciliation rule cannot
see this: those rows carry a real `dataSource`.

The discriminator is the SILENCE, not the age of the current row. A gate on the
current row's feed age reads the time of day instead — an evening Halloween event
is twelve hours stale at three in the afternoon and a water park in October is
forty-five days stale — and at six hours it would have taken 892 of the 1,107
CLOSED readings in open parks, 80.6 %. "No observation in 14 days, no hard
marker, park operating" takes 64.

`attractions.source_last_fresh_at` now holds the newest reading that was an
observation, stamped nightly by `detect-silent-sources` (02:45), and
`readsUnknownFromSilentSource` reads it on the park, ride and favourites
payloads. A frozen OPERATING is left alone — that is the wait-time side of the
same column and has its own ticket. `closedAttractions` and the analytics
counters now leave an unknown ride out of both halves instead of counting it as
closed while the list beside it serves it as unknown.
