### Fixed — the nightly downtime reconstruction finishes again, and can no longer hang

`reconstruct-downtime` failed every night from 2026-10-06, leaving
`attraction_outages`, `attraction_downtime_profiles`, `park_downtime_coverage`
and `downtime_recovery_curves` frozen at 2026-10-05. The closure-gap statement
as PAR-157 rewrote it never finished over the nightly 60-day scan: the planner
estimates one row for every CTE after `blind_parks` and stacks the final
aggregates as nested loops (14 s over 3 days, past a 300 s timeout over 10).
It now runs with `enable_nestloop = off`, and `blind_parks`' evidence-hours
check is one grouped pass instead of a per-park subquery that, with nested
loops off, rescanned all of `attraction_exposure_days` 192 times. Against
production: 5.7 s over the full 60 days, where the pre-PAR-157 version took
~110 s; same 92 blind parks, same 4404 rows.

Every statement of the job also runs under `SET LOCAL` deadlines now
(`statement_timeout` 10 min for reads, growing with the scan for staged fills;
5 min for writes; `idle_in_transaction_session_timeout` 2 min on the write
transaction). Before, there was none: the hung statement held the queue's only
slot until a deploy killed the process, and kept running on the server after
that, holding its snapshot. `statement_timeout` is the limit that protects the
rest of the database; the 30 s `lock_timeout` only makes the job fail fast
instead of waiting behind somebody else's ACCESS EXCLUSIVE. Hand runs from the
admin endpoint are enqueued with `attempts: 1`. The retention prune's "rows
pruned" log line, which could never fire, now reads TypeORM's
`[rows, rowCount]` correctly.
