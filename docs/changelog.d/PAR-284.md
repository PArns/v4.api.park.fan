### Changed — the curated-works-window E2E fixture binds its as-of the way the only caller does

`outage-curated-window-operating-day.e2e-spec.ts` held the statement's window end
and as-of apart: `WIN_END` was 2026-06-17T00:00+02:00 and `AS_OF` was
2026-06-16T03:00+02:00, 21 hours earlier. The only caller binds them to one
instant — `downtime-reconstruction.processor.ts` hands `asOf` to both `$3` and
`$4` — so the fixture fed `OUTAGE_INTERVALS_SQL` readings from after its own
as-of, a state production cannot reach. The after-hours fixture's 10:30 and 11:30
readings on the 16th both sat past the old as-of. `AS_OF` is now `WIN_END`, and
the comment names the caller as the reason rather than explaining the artefact
the gap produced.

The after-hours anchor also pins its `endReason` now. It checked the start and
the operating day but left the end open, and the measured value is `gap` rather
than `recovered`: `lost_sight` weighs the spell's OPEN minutes against its
observed ones, the night is not open and so does not count, and what breaks the
10-minute allowance of `MAX_UNOBSERVED_MINUTES_IN_SPELL` is the half hour between
the park opening at 10:00 and the first morning reading at 10:30 — 90 open
minutes against 60 observed. That is a regression claim of its own: the interval
is reported as censored, not cut short at the observation hole.

sits after a recovery for the after-hours ride, which is not seen running again
until 11:30 the next morning. Truncating the interval cannot send `startOpDay`
NULL, because a spell overlapping no window has zero operating minutes and leaves

`toHaveLength(1)` already catches it. And it is the 10:30 DOWN reading, not the
11:30 one, that carries the interval past the 16th's 10:00 opening; `start_op_day`
reads only the spell's bounds, so reclassifying the end does not move the day.
