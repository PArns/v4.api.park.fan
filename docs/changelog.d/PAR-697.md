### Fixed — a past day the operator calls shut, which the rides measurably worked through, is published as an operating day

Walibi Holland's 2026-04-18 had 16 rides reporting waits from 10:00 to 18:00 and
a park-level `CLOSED` entry over it. Since PAR-692 the statistics drop exactly
such a day, so the calendar and the statistics agreed — on the wrong answer.
Measured against production over `2025-12-24` … `2026-10-07`, **6,174** park-level
shut days carry **1,238** with any measured activity, and **210** of those look
like an operating day rather than like a feed artefact.

For a **strictly past** day the measurement now wins: the calendar publishes it
as `OPERATING` with `isEstimated: true` and its reconstructed hours, and the four
callers of the shut-day rule count it as a measuring day. Future days and today
are untouched — a future day has no measurement to weigh against the feed, and a
day in progress has no shape yet.

**What counts as "looks like an operating day"** is one rule in
`src/parks/utils/measured-operation.gate.ts`, four conditions, each with the
production day it was measured to reject: at least 3 different wait values
(Walibi Holland's 2026-09-11 had 14 rides reporting the same number at the same
minute), a block of 4–14 hours from the day's first qualifying reading to its
last (that same day was a 55-minute burst), nothing at all in the dead hours
02:00–05:59 park-local (352 of 551 candidate days carried waits through the
night), and readings whose upstream `lastUpdated` trails their own timestamp by
no more than 60 minutes (PAR-748 measured the night-time rows: 0 heartbeats,
46.7 % from a frozen feed). The last condition both drops days and admits them —
it costs 4 and gains 29, because removing a stale row can shorten a 15-hour
block or clear the single night reading the day failed on.

**The verdict is stored, not computed per read.** The rule has to reach raw
`queue_data` for the day it judges, and in the read path that cost the
ML-accuracy query a factor of 5.2 (1.4 s → 7.4 s) while a set-based CTE timed
out. The new `park_day_operations` table holds one row per (park, park-local
day) — the verdict, the derived hours it was taken from, and when it was taken —
written by the `park-day-operation` queue: `calculate-yesterday-park-day-operation`
nightly at 4:45 (after the 4:30 hourly-history rollup the hours are read from,
before the 5:00 downtime job that reads the same chunks), and
`backfill-park-day-operation` for an explicit range. The five readers do a
primary-key lookup instead: measured against production, the ML-accuracy query
runs 4.53 s without it and 5.08 s with it, inside the 4.28–5.65 s spread of the
unchanged query itself.

**A day with no row keeps the operator's entry.** The fill of the past runs as
its own job after the deploy, in portions, so for a while most days have no
verdict — and a park added later always will. The readers ask for a row with
`measured_operation` rather than for the absence of a `false` one, which makes
the old rule the fallback by construction.
