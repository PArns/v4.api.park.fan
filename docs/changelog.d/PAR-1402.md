### Performance — trip regions keep their calendar by month, so every window reads the months another route built

`trip_planner_ops` showed `/best-days` never reading a cached region calendar: 855 of 855 builds
missed it between 21 October and 19 November. That figure was the metric, not the code. A
best-days row recorded the calendar's state as its answer cache, because the route had no answer
cache of its own, so the built rows were by definition the ones whose calendar missed. Its real
reuse sits in the answer column: Orlando 125 hits, 320 joined and 266 builds. The builds were slow
for a reason of their own. The region calendar was one Redis entry per exact window,
`trip-regions:calendar:v2:{region}:{from}:{to}`, kept an hour, and park.fan refetches each hub
window once an hour, just as the entry has expired. A month page, the 30 days on the hub and the
year `/when` reads shared nothing, even where their days were the same.

The region calendar is now kept by month from the region's today,
`trip-regions:calendar:v3:{region}:{today}:{YYYY-MM}`, still an hour, and every window is cut from
the months it touches (`calendarMonths`, `joinCalendarMonths`). A day is priced from its own
reference span, so a window cut from the months is the same as one read alone; the new spec pins
that. The months a read misses are built together from one read over all of them, and a request
that needs a month another request is building waits for it. `/when`'s year therefore reads the
months that `/calendar`, `/best-days`, `/plan` and `/plan/ideas` built within the hour, and those
routes read the twelve `/when` built. The search of a 10-day trip on 7 parks takes 0.15–0.3 s on
production's year ([when to go](frontend/trip-regions-when.md#how-it-is-computed)), so nearly all
of the 13 s `/when` took at p90 in Orlando was that year read, rebuilt for almost every answer.

`/best-days` keeps its answer for an hour, `trip-regions:best-days:v1:{region}:{today}:{from}:{to}:{limit}`.
The hub's 30 days asked without dates and the same days asked with them are one entry, and so are
`limit` left out and `limit=5`. Its metric rows now hold the answer's cache in `cache` and the
calendar's in `calendar_cache`, as `/plan` does; rows from before the deploy still hold the
calendar's state in both. No Redis key holds the client's release `v`.

A cold read builds the whole months it touches, so a cold 10-day plan now reads up to two months
where it read 10 days. Building the hub's months right after the calendar warmup would only pay if
they lived longer than an hour, and that needs evicting them on a schedule sync and on a season
edit, which this change does not do
([caching](frontend/trip-regions.md#caching-and-cost)).
