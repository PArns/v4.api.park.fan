### Added — the season level: a running park season sets a ride's served daily level

No model reads `park_seasons`, so a season was invisible to the calendar until its waits had
worked their way into the models' history. On 2026-11-14, the first day of Phantasialand's
Wintertraum, the calendar read "very low" for every day to 18 December with a headliner peak of
10–25 minutes, the Advent Saturdays included, while the day itself averaged 35 minutes.

With `SEASON_LEVEL=true` (on in `docker-compose.production.yml`), `getServingDailyPredictions`
replaces a ride's daily level on a day of a running season — kind `halloween`, `christmas`,
`winter`, `summer_nights` or `special_event`, status `confirmed` or `announced`, the season's
listed `dates` or else its range — by the median of the ride's measured daily P90 on that
season's days already over: of the same day type (weekend or weekday) once it has three days,
else of all its days once those are three. Until then the model's number stays, so a season's
first days are not covered. The calendar, `/plan/day` day levels and the yearly view all read it.

It is the weekday average restricted to the season and is not measured against the models. A
replaced row is its own source, `season`: the lead snapshots record its error by lead from the
first night, and the forward archive no longer pairs the daily candidates against the served
level on season days, which leaves fewer paired days at parks in a long season for the decisions
dated December 2026. Rollback: `SEASON_LEVEL=false`. No entity change: both `source` columns are
`varchar` and wide enough ([season level](ml/season-level.md)).
