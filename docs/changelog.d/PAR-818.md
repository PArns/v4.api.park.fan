### Fixed — CatBoost reads the park's clock in four places that read UTC

- **Daily peak hours.** `DAILY_PEAK_HOURS=12,14,16` were set on the UTC base
  time, so only London in winter was asked about its afternoon: Los Angeles
  read 04–09 local, New York 07–12, Shanghai 20/22/00, Tokyo and Seoul
  21/23/01, Brisbane 22/00/02. The hours are now park-local, the per-day
  maximum is grouped by the park-local date, and the surviving daily row is
  published at park-local noon of its day, so every consumer that reads the day
  off the ISO string (calendar, `/plan/day`, the TFT merge) and every
  `DATE(predictedTime AT TIME ZONE p.timezone)` agree on it. This does not
  remove CatBoost-daily's low bias, which comes mainly from serving a
  median-like quantile against a P90 truth.
- **Hourly weather.** Open-Meteo answers park-local hours without an offset
  (`timezone:"auto"`) and `predict.py` joined them as UTC — each hour's weather
  two (Europe) to nine (Japan) hours off. `WeatherService.getHourlyForecast` now
  hands the ML service UTC instants (new cache key `weather:hourly:v2:park:*`),
  which also puts the holiday flags on that payload on the right day. Training
  joins `weather_data` on the park-local date instead of the UTC one.
- **Occupancy profile.** The historical `(DOW, hour)` profile is keyed on the
  park's wall clock but was looked up with the UTC weekday and hour. Its cache
  key carried the request time to the microsecond, so the one-hour cache never
  hit and every `/predict` re-ran an 8-week `queue_data` scan; the cut-off is
  now floored to the hour and expired entries are evicted.
- **Stored-prediction gating.** `storePredictions` gated each prediction by the
  schedule of its UTC date: a 20:00 slot in Los Angeles was checked against
  tomorrow's schedule. It now uses the park-local date.
