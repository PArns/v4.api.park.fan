### Fixed — `/plan/day` states who may ride

The trip planner asks two questions about the party once per day: how tall the
shortest rider is, and whether the group would rather stay dry. Both are
answered against `minimumHeight` and `mayGetWet`, and the planner knows those
two facts from `/plan/day` alone — it does not fetch forty attraction payloads
for one day. The endpoint sent neither, so every ride came back without an
answer and every ride was flagged as fine. Measured against production on
2026-09-20: Phantasialand's day carried 33 rides and Europa-Park's 40, with
**zero** entries holding either field, while the attraction payload for
`chiapas-die-wasserbahn` states `minimumHeight: 130` and `mayGetWet: true`.

The cause is one `select`. `PlanDayService.attractions()` names the columns it
needs, the four height and wet columns were not among them, and the two mappers
that assemble a ride (`forecastRides` and `observedRides`) never set the fields.
Nothing failed while they were missing: a resolver reading an unselected column
sees `undefined`, which is indistinguishable from a ride nobody has curated.

What the frontend did with that silence is the visible half. `partyFlags()`
returned `NONE` for every ride, so the search list drew neither a droplet nor a
height, the water-ride marker on a placed block could not fire, and
`headlinersToAdd` filtered on `!flags.wet && !flags.tooShort` with nothing to
filter — which is why "optimise the day" planned Chiapas into a day whose owner
had asked to stay dry.

**Both fields are omitted where nothing is known**, and that is deliberate
rather than incidental. They are resolved through `resolveCuratedFacts`, so the
curated cell wins over the synced one and a curated height of `0` means "no
minimum at all" rather than a 0 cm limit — it resolves to null and the field is
absent, because there is no threshold left to flag anybody against. A curated
`false` on the wet flag is a real statement and does travel. The direction that
had to be closed is the other one: upstream populates `may_get_wet` for a few
dozen of ~7000 attractions, so a `false` for "nobody checked" would tell a
family that asked to stay dry that every unflagged ride is safe, and a `0` for
"no height recorded" would tell them every ride is. That is
[an absent fact becoming a confident one](../rules/absent-facts.md), one payload
further out than the rule usually bites.

`minimumHeightUnit` stays off this payload. The column is a display decision —
US parks post 52″, and `minimumHeight` is centimetres on every row whatever the
sign says — and a planner compares the number against a child's height instead
of printing it.

The spec covers both mappers, because a past day is built by the second one and
the first draft of this change would have touched only the first. Run against
the unmodified service, four of its six cases go red; the two that do not are
the absence assertions, which cannot fail today and stand as guards against a
future `false` or `0` being put there.
