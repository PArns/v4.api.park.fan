### Added — trip regions: the Orlando parks side by side and an optimized vacation plan

Routes for park.fan's Orlando hub and vacation planner
([trip regions](frontend/trip-regions.md)). `GET /v1/trip-regions/orlando` lists
the region's 16 parks in their resorts, five hotel areas with their perks, and
the drive between every pair from OpenStreetMap (OSRM, free flow) with each
resort's own transport beside it; it needs no key. `…/calendar` puts every
park's days side by side for up to 92 days and a year ahead: hours, the early
and evening slots read off the schedule feed, the crowd level, the headliners'
mean expected wait and where it comes from (`forecast`, `long_range`, or the
same weekday a year back past the forecast). A curated closure season closes a
day the feed is silent about, and a ticketed season supplies a party night
the feed does not list. `…/plan` places up to 16 visits on a trip of up to 21 days by a
min-cost assignment over resort blocks, hop days and rest-day patterns, with
hotel perks, party nights, locks and repeat visits, and says when the result is
provably the best. Both need a data key and are kept an hour in Redis.

One day cost prices every day ([how a day is priced](frontend/trip-regions.md#how-a-day-is-priced)):
the headliners' unrounded wait times how much shorter the day guest's day is than the park's usual
day around that date, so a party night that sends day guests home at 18:00 is not the best pick on
its minutes alone. The calendar names each resort's best park of the day (`best`, `bestWhy`) and
marks a day that `closesEarly`; a fourth keyed route, `…/best-days`, ranks each park's quietest and
busiest days on that cost. The plan takes `fireworks=want|any` (in place of `parties`), keeps a
park's visits a day apart, keeps resort blocks by default, gives spare days to second visits with
`split=auto`, and reports `orderMatters` and the measured `expectedError`. `pnpm trip-regions:dev`
runs all four routes on production data before they are deployed.

The schedule sync keys an event row by its start too, so Walt Disney World's Early Entry and a
party night on one day are both kept; until now one replaced the other, alternating between syncs
([schedule sync](architecture/schedule-sync-and-calendar.md)).

The calendar warmup now takes the region's parks to six months ahead and keeps
the past months their last-year days read as week-long snapshots
([caching](architecture/caching-strategy.md#what-the-trip-regions-add)).
`lastYearDays` moved to the shared date utilities.

Parky plans Orlando trips ([assistant](frontend/assistant.md#orlando-the-vacation-planner-in-the-chat),
prompt version 48). `plan_vacation` runs the plan service directly and retells the itinerary day by
day: parks with their headliners' wait, crowd level, basis, hours on the park's clock, perks,
events and travel, the reasons and warnings as short phrases (a `last_year` day reads "estimate
from last year, not a forecast"), the totals, and `hubUrl`, the hub with the plan in its query
under the plan route's own parameter names. `get_park_events` lists the curated seasons with
their status and, for Orlando, the days with Early Entry, Early Park Admission, Extended Evening
Hours and party nights with the day guests' close; `get_travel_time` gives a leg's drive, where it
parks and the resort's own transport, and says there is no data outside Orlando. `open_page`
opens the hub, with a plan from its `hubUrl`, and `plan_trip` with `days` reserves a trip across
parks at once (`planner_reserve_trip`). The page context names the hub and its month pages, and
12 eval cases (German and English) cover the routing.

**The day optimizer moved from the browser into the API.** `POST
/v1/parks/:continent/:country/:city/:parkSlug/plan/optimize`, behind a data key and answered
`no-store`, takes a planned day as park.fan's planner stores it and answers with the order that
costs the least queueing: `day` re-orders, `headliners` adds the headliners the party can ride,
`fit` answers the fit assistant, and a day that cannot hold what was asked answers `needs-fit`
instead of a plan. The server plans against its own cached `/plan/day` plus the rides in works, so
no forecast leaves for a client to search over, and park.fan and Parky now share one engine. The
search (beam width 192, up to 24 stops) is park.fan's `lib/planner/optimize.ts` and `fit.ts`
ported unchanged to `src/planner/day-optimizer/` and run in worker threads, since a full fit
answer is several searches of about a tenth of a second each. The browser's 187 test vectors
pass as Jest specs, and 66 recorded browser answers over three real days and eighteen synthetic
ones replay to the same orders, start minutes, scores and levers. Contract:
[`/plan/optimize`](frontend/plan-day/optimize.md).
