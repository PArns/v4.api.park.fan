### Fixed — ML features are computed in the park's local time, not in UTC

`convert_to_local_time` built `local_timestamp` by copying the UTC column and then
writing each park's converted values into it with `df.loc[idx, ...]`. A pandas
datetime column carries exactly one timezone, so pandas 2.2.3 converted every write
straight back to UTC — no warning, no error. Every feature derived from it was the
UTC one while the code, the comments and the column name all said local: `hour`,
`hour_sin/cos`, `day_of_week`, `is_weekend`, `month`, `day_of_year`, `season` and
`date_local`, in training and inference alike.

`date_local` is the expensive one. It is the join key for the holiday and school
holiday lookups, for `downtime_minutes_today`, and for the park schedule — so a row
at 21:30 in New York was matched against the NEXT day's calendar entry and the next
day's opening hours, reading `is_park_open = 0` and `time_since_park_open_mins = 0`
in the middle of a running evening. Tokyo and Sydney had the mirror image before
their morning opening.

Measured on 300000 production rows, comparing the two implementations through
`add_time_features`: `hour` differs on 99.8 % of rows, `date_local` and
`day_of_week` on 3.1 %, `is_weekend` on 0.8 %.

The fix converts once per TIMEZONE and stores the park's wall-clock reading tz-naive,
which is the only shape one column can hold. That makes `local_timestamp` a reading
rather than an instant, so the two places that compared it against a `TIMESTAMPTZ`
schedule now compare `timestamp` (UTC) instead, while the join keeps the park-local
date: the schedule join in `features.py` and `add_time_since_park_open`, whose
`parkOpeningTimes` arrive as ISO instants from `MlService`. `predict.py` already
compared the right column and only carried a comment saying otherwise.

Note for the deploy: the serving model is trained on the UTC features. Until it is
retrained, it is served features it has never seen — deploy and retraining belong in
the same window.
