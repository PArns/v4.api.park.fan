### Fixed — the analytics counters stop counting retired attractions

`retired_at` promises that a retired ride leaves the park listings, the counts
and search. The three counters in `AnalyticsService` were the part that had not
followed: `getParkStatistics`, `getAttractionCounts` and `getGlobalRealtimeStats`
all read `FROM attractions` without the predicate.

Measured against production on 2026-09-17: 49 retired rows in 13 parks, every
one of them through `attractionIsOutOfSeason` and therefore fully counted. The
error did not land where it looked like it would. `explicitly_closed_count` was
already right — a retired ride is not polled, so it has no fresh reading to be
counted closed by — which means the whole of it landed in
`operatingAttractions`, the difference of the two, and that is the field
`LocationService.getNearbyParks` publishes. Universal Studios Singapore reported
17 operating rides at 06:00 local with the park shut; all 17 were retired rows.

The global board counted the same rows twice over, once in `total` and once in
`closed`, because a missing reading counts as closed there. And
`openAttractionsCount` counted a retired ride as open for as long as its last
reading stayed inside the 24-hour window: 31 of the 49 rows had an `OPERATING`
row in the 24 hours after their own retirement.

`getAttractionCounts` needed both of its reads changed rather than the one the
issue named, because its `closed` is `total - operating` and no retired row
carries an `OPERATING` reading — filtering only the operating half would have
moved nothing.

The two parameterless statements behind the global board are now exported as
`GLOBAL_PARK_STATS_SQL` and `OPEN_ATTRACTIONS_COUNT_SQL`, so
`test/e2e/analytics-counts-retired.e2e-spec.ts` runs them against a real
database instead of asserting their text. One of them needed it: the new
predicate sits in front of an `OR`, where a missing pair of brackets would let a
retired ride with a live reading back into the count without breaking anything
a string assertion could see.
