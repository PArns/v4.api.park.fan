### Added — level-driver analysis and the `driver_level` model in ml-bench

`python -m mlbench drivers` measures how much of a day's level is explainable in
advance: per ride-day and lead, `log(true daily P90 / naive level)` against
weekday, holidays (own and neighbour regions, OR semantics), bridge days,
published opening hours (only where the schedule row was written before the
origin), ticketed events, season start/end and — separately, as ORACLE —
weather, with walk-forward validation by origin month. Out of sample the drivers
explain 12–16 % of a ride-day's and 19–28 % of a park-day's squared log
deviation (level MAE −4…−8 % per ride, −7…−12 % per park, d1–d60) and lift the
within-month day ranking from 0.27 to 0.34 at d1. The gradient-boosted version
is a harness plug-in (`driver_level`), scored like every other model.

Scored over all 231 origins of the 20261009 export, `driver_level` is the first
model in the benchmark that beats climatology on 15-min slot MAE at d7–d10 on
all rides and at d14–d60 on ex-ante busy rides, where the baselines left
climatology unbeaten: it extends the usable horizon from d6 to d10 on all rides and from d7 to
d60 on ex-ante busy rides, extends the planner optimiser's from d7 to d14, and
is the only daily level source that wins the crowd bucket (D6) and the day
comparison (D7) at every lead from d8 to d90. It closes 8.5 % of the d1 level
gap between the served H5 profile and a perfect level (16.5 % on busy rides).
It is **not** recommended for serving yet: it loses the best-time surface (D3)
against H5, it is worse than H5 throughout summer, and it gains nothing in Asia.
See docs/ml/level-drivers.md.
