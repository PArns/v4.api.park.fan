### Added — level-driver analysis and the `driver_level` model in ml-bench

`python -m mlbench drivers` measures how much of a day's level is explainable in
advance: per ride-day and lead, `log(true daily P90 / naive level)` against
weekday, holidays (own and neighbour regions, OR semantics), bridge days,
published opening hours (only where the schedule row was written before the
origin), ticketed events, season start/end and — separately, as ORACLE —
weather, with walk-forward validation by origin month. Out of sample the drivers
explain 12–16 % of a ride-day's and 19–28 % of a park-day's squared log
deviation (level MAE −4…−8 % per ride, −7…−12 % per park, d1–d60) and lift the
within-month day ranking from 0.27 to 0.34 at d1. The gradient-boosted version
is a harness plug-in (`driver_level`), scored like every other model.

Scored over all 231 origins of the 20261009 export, `driver_level` has the
lowest 15-min slot MAE of every competing model at every lead d1–d60 on both
all rides and ex-ante busy rides — measured as a paired margin against each
named baseline individually (`snaive7`, `wt_med`, `clim`, `h5`, TFT×H5), so the
result does not depend on which naive the harness picks as its reference. It
extends the usable horizon on busy rides from d7 (H5) to d60, extends the
planner optimiser's from d7 to d14, and is the only daily level source that wins
the crowd bucket (D6) and the day comparison (D7) at every lead from d8 to d90.
It closes 8.5 % of the d1 level gap between the served H5 profile and a perfect
level (15–17 % on busy rides).

It is **shadow-only, not for serving**: it regresses `bestVisitTimes` (D3), a
surface the frontend ships today, against both climatology and H5 and under both
bootstrap units, and it is worse than H5 throughout summer at every lead. It
also gains nothing in Asia, is worse than naive at d90 on all rides, and loses
the day ranking at d180. See docs/ml/level-drivers.md.
