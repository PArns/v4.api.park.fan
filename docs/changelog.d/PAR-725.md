### Added — a rule files Halloween mazes as `MAZE`

`attraction_kind` is a hand-written verdict, and until now every Halloween maze needed one typed for it — once per park, and again next season, because ThemeParks.wiki hands a maze a new entity id every autumn. `detect-seasonal` now files them itself. A row becomes `MAZE` when all three hold: the resolved name carries an event-horror word at the start of a word, the row resolves as seasonal (curated before detected), and its season touches September–November or a month a non-cancelled `halloween` park season covers. The rule writes where `attraction_kind IS NULL` and nowhere else, so an editor's verdict — including "this is a RIDE" on something whose name sounds like a maze — is never taken back.

The token list is calibrated against all 7,575 production attraction names rather than guessed, and two measurements shaped it. The pattern anchors at the start of a word: matched anywhere, `fear` claimed Liseberg's AtmosFear (a 116 m drop tower) and `evil` claimed all eight `D-evil` rows — Jersey Devil Coaster, Dare Devil Dive, Daredevil Falls, Lil' Devil Coaster. And `voodoo` is deliberately not a token: all four rows carrying it are rides, including a Kentucky Kingdom water slide. Anchored and without it, the rule selected 9 rows in 2 parks against production on 2026-10-06, all 9 genuine mazes; matched freely it selected 11, two of them wrong.

The decision runs in TypeScript over loaded rows instead of inside the `UPDATE`, so the match has one implementation and no SQL twin to drift from `resolveCuratedFacts` — and the step sits inside `detect-seasonal` rather than on a cron of its own, because it reads the two columns the steps above it have just written.

What the rule does not reach is the season rather than the name: of the nine mazes already filed by hand at Movie Park Germany, seven resolve as not seasonal, because a maze that runs twelve evenings in October never collects the 20 observed `OPERATING` rows `detect-seasonal` asks for. Those stay hand work (PAR-727), and a pattern that keeps turning up there belongs in the token list.
