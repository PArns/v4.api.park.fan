### Added — `/plan/day` in quarter-hours, composed from the H5 profile (behind a flag)

The planner's composed days stretched a year's hourly P50 profile until its
maximum equalled the day level. Scored on 15-minute truth in the ML review's
offline benchmark, that is worse than a plain weekday-median profile from d3 on
(+0.5 to +1.1 min MAE; first-hour MAE 6.50 against 3.87, bias +2.3, because a
median profile with its top pinned to a P90-like level lifts every hour). With
`PLAN_DAY_H5_COMPOSER=true` composed hours now come from the H5 profile — the
ride's last 56 days, its first hour aligned to the published opening (rope-drop
ramp), its last hour to the closing, hourly medians interpolated onto
quarter-hours in between — scaled by the RATIO of the TFT day level to the ride's
median daily P90 over the same window, the opening hour unscaled. CatBoost's
daily level is never applied (it made the benchmark worse: +2.6 min, bias −5.7);
without a TFT level the plain profile is served. Days past 90, rides with no
profile, and a failed profile read keep the old composer. Every ride with a
15-minute answer gains `slots[]` (`minute`, `start`, whole-minute `wait`,
`source`) — the model's own quarter-hours on measured hours, H5 on composed ones
— and a composed H5 hour is the mean of its slots; `hours[]` keeps its shape.
`composer` (`peak_scaled` / `h5` / `h5_tft`) is on every composed ride, and the
forward archive stores slot rides at 15 minutes under new source codes `h` and
`t`, so the board scores old and new side by side. Profiles are read from
`attraction_hourly_history` and `schedule_entries` (never `queue_data`) under
statement limits and cached per park-day in Redis for six hours. Contract and
frontend changes: `docs/frontend/plan-day-endpoint.md` §2a.
