### Added — a ride that stands still gets the "how much longer" estimate, and the nightly closure history carries the early-end filter

A `closed_gap` outage (a ride in a park whose feed never says DOWN that stopped inside opening hours) now carries `estimate` like a reported one, read off its own recovery curve. It was withheld because that curve could only have been fit on closures that came back the same operating day: `CLOSURE_GAP_INTERVALS_SQL` returned nothing else, so the population had zero censoring and lost every ride that broke in the afternoon and stayed shut.

`CLOSURE_GAP_INTERVALS_SQL` now keeps those closures. It reads status changes (`chg`) rather than adjacent readings, and a closure that does not come back the same operating day is followed to the ride's next status for at most `LIVE_LOOKBACK_HOURS` (26), the longest the live line can stand. It is stored `recovered`, `reclassified`, `ongoing` or `window_edge`, with `operating_minutes` summed over the park's windows, so a night is not counted. The processor writes those columns as the statement returns them instead of stamping every closure `recovered` with wall minutes.

Four filters change for both shapes. Every closure must start inside a published window with `MIN_PARK_MINUTES_LEFT` of it left; the live line has always asked that, and a gap in the last hour is short by construction. Simultaneity counts every closure of the park in that minute, not only the gaps that came back. Regularity counts both shapes. `MAX_EARLY_END_SHARE` now runs in the nightly statement too: the share of a ride's active days that end in a closure that did not come back, which needs no per-day comparison against the close (todo.md §2f).

`RECOVERY_CURVE_SQL` builds a curve per signal instead of `down` only. For `closed_gap` a `reclassified` end is censored, because a closure turning into REFURBISHMENT is the ride still not running. `CURRENT_CLOSURE_GAP_SQL` returns `elapsedOperatingMinutes` on the same clock, and `AttractionOutageService` reads the estimate for both signals through one helper (`estimatesFor`). `OUTAGE_SCAN_START_SQL` looks at `down` rows only: a closure is cut off after 26 hours, and its censored rows keep `ended_at = NULL`, which would otherwise pin every nightly scan to twice the window.

Covered by `test/e2e/closure-standing.e2e-spec.ts` (nine cases on TimescaleDB, from a closure that runs again the next morning to the curve split by signal) and new shape assertions in `closure-gap.sql.spec.ts`, `downtime-recovery.service.spec.ts` and `attraction-outage.service.spec.ts`. Not measured against production: the statement's run time over 21 and 30 days, and how many stored rows the new filters remove (PAR-157).

New in the same pull request: `notRunToday: { lastRunAt }` on the park payload's attractions and on the attraction detail, for a ride that reads CLOSED while its park has been open for 15 minutes and has had no OPERATING reading since the middle of the night, halfway between the previous operating day's last close and today's first opening (`NOT_RUN_TODAY_SQL`, `AttractionOutageService.getNotRunToday`). The first version started the day at the previous close itself, so one OPERATING reading just after the close made a ride that had stood all day count as having run. That is the likely reason Bolles Riesenrad in Phantasialand, which ran until Sunday's close and stood all Monday, got no line on 2026-10-05; its readings were not checked in production. A midday break no longer starts a new day either. `lastRunAt` is the end of its last run, clipped to the close of that day's window, and the field is absent when the last run is not on one of the six days before today in the park's calendar (seven days back would be named by today's weekday), for a ride out of season or in a works period, and beside an `outage`. It is its own field rather than a third outage signal because the feed cannot say why the ride has not run. See `docs/frontend/not-run-today.md`; pinned by `test/e2e/not-run-today.e2e-spec.ts`.
